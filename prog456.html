Purpose

The curriculum in Criminal Justice has been developed and maintained in cooperation with state and local correctional and police officials. The Criminal Justice curriculum was designed to provide a broad foundation which will prepare the student to enter any of the varied fields of corrections, probation/parole and/or law enforcement, or to advance professionally within them.

Occupational Objectives

Students receiving an associate degree in Criminal Justice will have a wide variety of occupational choices, some of which are listed below: Local Correctional Officer, State Correctional Officer, Federal Correctional Officer, State Probation Officer, Parole Officer, Federal Probation/Parole Officer, City or Town Police Officer, Deputy Sheriff, State Police, Federal Law Enforcement, Commercial and Industrial Security Officer. Additional professional training will be required for some of the above careers.

Program Student Learning Outcomes

In addition to the VCCS general education outcomes and core competencies, students will be able to:

  • Demonstrate ethical standards during mock pre-employment interviews, work and life situations.
  • Demonstrate policy analysis by following departmental policies, changing policies and making recommendations.
  • Explain the basic guarantees of liberty in the U.S. Constitution such as the rights of free speech, press, assembly, as well as procedures guarantees to counsel, jury trial, and habeas corpus.
  • Demonstrate an understanding of the scope of crime and various theories to explain the causation of crime and criminality.
  • Explain the concepts of normal and abnormal behavior, including focuses on the psychological and sociological aspects of criminal and other deviant behavior patterns. Demonstrate critical and analytical thinking and ability to write clearly and accurately about the administration of justice process using an appropriate vocabulary.
  • Students completing courses from the Corrections Science pathway will also demonstrate an understanding of the United States criminal justice system including the major system components – law enforcement, judiciary, and corrections.

Admission Requirements: General Admission to the college is required. In addition, entry into this curriculum requires proficiency in high school English and mathematics.

Students who have successfully completed the Virginia State Police Academy or a Virginia Department of Criminal Justice Services approved Training Academy for law enforcement or corrections officers may be granted credit upon acceptance into the program.

Special Conditions: Students should be aware that most criminal justice agencies require excellent moral character, and students with certain misdemeanor or felony convictions may have limited opportunities in the criminal justice field.

Program Requirements: Approximately one-half of the curriculum will include courses in Corrections or Law Enforcement with the remaining courses in related subjects. Instruction will include both theoretical concepts and practical applications needed for future success in Corrections or Law Enforcement.
